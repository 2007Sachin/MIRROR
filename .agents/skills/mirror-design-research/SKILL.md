# Mirror Design Research

Use this skill before major landing-page or product-positioning changes.

## Research order

1. Define the user decision the page or screen must support.
2. Gather current primary or high-authority sources first: labor-market data, recruiting research, academic interview research, official product docs, and reputable UX/design references.
3. Separate facts from inference. Record source, date, population/sample, and what the evidence actually supports.
4. Translate only supported findings into Mirror messaging. Do not imply that Mirror solves a problem merely because the problem exists.
5. Compare relevant products for information architecture and interaction patterns, not for visual copying.
6. Convert findings into explicit design hypotheses: message, hierarchy, visual proof, trust, CTA, palette, motion.
7. Validate those hypotheses against current Mirror capabilities and screenshots before implementation.

## Source quality

Prefer primary reports and peer-reviewed research. Treat vendor surveys, resume sites, and opinion articles as directional unless methodology is clear. Avoid unsupported social-media statistics.

## Data-to-copy rule

Research statistics belong in proof/problem sections or cited research notes, not automatically in the hero. The hero should express the product mechanism in plain language.

## Competitive research rule

Do not copy a competitor's composition, palette, illustrations, or proprietary interface. Extract reusable principles such as hierarchy, clarity, product proof, trust placement, and interaction density.

## Design synthesis

For each research round produce:
- 3–5 strongest externally supported insights;
- what each insight means for Mirror;
- what it does NOT prove;
- one content/design implication;
- one risk of overstating it.

Then write an implementation brief that explicitly distinguishes `research-backed`, `product-backed`, and `design hypothesis` decisions.
